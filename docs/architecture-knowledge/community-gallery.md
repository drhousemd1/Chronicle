# Overview

## Community Gallery at a Glance

![Community Gallery showing its discovery controls and published story results](knowledge-asset:community-gallery-overview)

### Chronicle’s landing page for discovering published stories

Community Gallery is Chronicle’s public landing page and catalog of creator-published stories. It gives visitors one place to discover roleplay experiences, judge whether a story interests them, understand its content before entering it, and choose what they want to do next. The Gallery begins several user journeys, but it does not own the later work that happens in My Stories, Story Builder, Chat Interface, Chat History, or Public Profile.

Only stories deliberately published from Story Builder belong in the Gallery. Private drafts, unpublished work, private conversations, and a creator’s editable working copy do not appear here. Publication creates a public-facing version for discovery while the underlying story remains owned and controlled by its creator.

Each publication appears as a cover-led story card. Before the user hovers over it, the card already shows the cover image, SFW or NSFW label, story title, a brief synopsis shortened to fit the card, view, like, save, and play totals, and the creator’s name. A purple pencil appears when the publisher allows other users to create their own editable copies. Hovering does not reveal more story information: it keeps the same card information visible and adds the Like, Save, and Play controls over the cover. Selecting the card itself opens the in-depth Story Detail modal.

The page is designed to take a person from a broad catalog to one informed decision. A visitor can narrow the results, open Story Detail for the complete public information, and then like, save, review, visit the creator, begin the path to a permitted custom copy, or start playing. Each action has a different result, and none gives the visitor authority to overwrite the creator’s original story.

### What a successful Gallery visit accomplishes

A successful visit ends with the user understanding both the story and the consequence of the action they are about to take. They should know whether the story is SFW or NSFW, what themes and warnings apply, who published it, whether customization is permitted, and whether the selected action will express interest, add the publication to Saved Stories, begin creation of a separate owned copy, or start a new conversation.

The Gallery must preserve that clarity while a person moves between broad discovery and detailed evaluation. Search, feed choice, and category criteria shape which publications are shown. Opening Story Detail supplies the fuller context needed to judge one result. Returning from Story Detail should leave the surrounding discovery context intact so the user can continue browsing rather than restart the search.

## Published Stories and Creator Ownership

### Story Builder controls what enters the Gallery

A story appears in Community Gallery only after its owner selects **Publish to Gallery** in Story Builder. Publication makes the story’s approved public information discoverable; it does not turn the creator’s private working story into a communal document. Story Builder remains the place where the creator edits the source and chooses whether it is published and whether other users are allowed to make separate editable copies.

Publication status is therefore different from story existence. A creator may own a story that has never appeared in the Gallery, and removing a publication from the Gallery does not mean deleting the underlying story. This distinction allows creators to control public availability without treating discovery, editing, and deletion as the same operation.

### Public engagement never transfers ownership or editing authority

Every visitor action is scoped to the publication or to the visitor’s own account. A like records appreciation. A review records that user’s assessment. A save creates a personal reference to the publication. A play creates a separate conversation. None of those actions makes the viewer the owner of the source story, changes the publisher, grants permission to edit the creator’s version, or allows one player’s conversation to alter the story for anyone else.

This ownership boundary is one of the Gallery’s central product rules. Chronicle must always distinguish the creator-owned story, its public Gallery publication, another user’s saved relationship, a separately cloned editable story, and a conversation created from the story. They may be connected by identity and history, but they are not interchangeable records.

### Creators retain control over publication and permitted reuse

The publisher decides whether the story remains available in Community Gallery and whether other users may create editable copies. Removing a story from the Gallery changes its public availability while preserving the creator’s source. Allowing edits communicates permission for another user to create a separate version; it does not open the original to shared editing.

Permission to customize is not permission to erase attribution or republish another creator’s work as if it were original. Chronicle must retain the relationship to the source publication and its creator when it creates an editable copy. The new owner may change their copy, but those changes belong to the copy and never flow back into the creator’s story.

Public engagement belongs to the publication. Likes, saves, plays, views, ratings, and reviews describe how the published story has been received. They do not become editing authority over the source, and they should not be confused with the private activity inside any conversation that a player later creates.

## Who Can Browse and Participate

### Discovery is available before sign-in

Community Gallery is Chronicle’s guest-accessible starting area. A signed-out visitor can browse the public catalog, search and filter it, open Story Detail, read public reviews, and follow a creator link to the public profile experience. This allows someone to understand Chronicle’s available stories before creating or entering an account.

Guest access is read-only. It exposes the published representation needed for discovery but does not expose private drafts, account-specific collections, private creator information, or other users’ conversations.

### Personal actions require an authenticated identity

Like, Save, Follow, and writing or changing a review create account-linked activity, so they require a signed-in user. When a visitor requests one of those actions without an account session, Chronicle asks them to authenticate and leaves the publication unchanged. Authentication is a prerequisite, not proof that the original action has already been completed.

Play has a more informative guest path. Selecting Play while signed out should carry the selected story into a read-only Chat Interface preview and then ask the visitor to sign in before they can send a message or begin saving a conversation. The preview lets the visitor see where the story will be played without pretending that a private playthrough has already started.

Once signed in, Chronicle can show whether that user has liked or saved a publication and can associate reviews, play activity, saved-story relationships, and conversations with the correct account. Following also depends on account identity because it uses the viewer’s creator relationships to produce a personalized discovery view.

### Adult-content access is a presentation decision

NSFW stories remain part of the discovery catalog when adult presentation is disabled, but explicit cover art is masked. Attempting to reveal or enter an NSFW story requires age confirmation. Chronicle keeps the age confirmation and the viewer’s Show or Hide NSFW preference as separate facts so a stored display preference cannot reveal adult material unless the age step has also been completed on that device.

The adult-content gate controls presentation and entry. It does not reclassify the publication, remove the creator’s content labels, or change ownership. Age confirmation and the Show or Hide NSFW preference are separate decisions: remembering a display preference must not bypass the confirmation required before revealing or entering adult content. If the viewer declines or closes the age prompt, the pending detail or play action is abandoned and the surrounding Gallery context remains available.

## From Discovery to an Informed Choice

### Users can narrow the catalog from several directions

The discovery experience supports a general public catalog, a recency-focused view, public popularity views based on Like, Save, and Play activity, stories from followed creators, text and hashtag search, and content-based filtering. The popularity views describe reception across the Gallery; they are not substitutes for the signed-in user’s own saved collection or play history. These criteria can work together so a user can begin with a general interest and progressively narrow the catalog without being forced into a single browsing method.

The result set should always represent the combination the user has chosen. Active criteria remain understandable and removable, clearing one criterion should not silently erase unrelated choices, and changing the discovery view should not change any publication or account data. These controls shape presentation; they do not mutate the stories being searched.

### Story cards provide the first level of information

The card is the Gallery’s quick-reading view. Its cover image establishes the visual identity of the story, while the labels and text at the bottom identify the story, summarize its premise, identify its creator, and show its public engagement totals. The synopsis is intentionally brief because it is constrained to the space available on the card; it is not a second or hidden description that appears on hover.

Hovering adds only the three quick actions: Like, Save, and Play. The title receives a blue highlight and the cover receives a small visual lift and zoom, but the title, shortened synopsis, counters, and creator remain the same information shown before hover. Selecting anywhere else on the card opens Story Detail rather than performing one of those quick actions.

### Story Detail opens the in-depth story modal

A result card is a compact introduction, not the complete public story record. Opening Story Detail gives the user the fuller information needed before saving or playing: a larger cover, the complete synopsis, creator identity, SFW or NSFW classification, whether editable copies are permitted, genres, character types and origin, trigger warnings and custom tags, visible characters, engagement totals, ratings, and written community reviews. Like, Save, and Play are also available inside the modal.

Story Detail keeps the user inside the Gallery while concentrating on one publication. The visitor can compare the story’s premise, content boundaries, creator information, and community response without first committing to a playthrough or adding the story to their account.

### Community signals support judgment without deciding for the user

Views, likes, saves, plays, ratings, and reviews describe different forms of community activity. A highly played story is not necessarily the most recently published; a frequently saved story is not necessarily part of the current viewer’s collection; and an average rating does not replace the story’s synopsis, classifications, or warnings. The Gallery presents these signals together so the user can interpret them rather than collapsing them into one unexplained measure of quality.

Reviews add both quick ratings and optional written context. They belong to the publication and help later visitors evaluate it, while the reviewer retains control over their own review through create, update, and delete actions.

## The Meaning of Gallery Actions

### Like and Review attach feedback to the publication

Like is a lightweight expression of appreciation. Review is a more deliberate assessment that can include Story and Spice ratings, additional category ratings, and written feedback. Both actions contribute to the public publication’s engagement context, and neither creates an owned story or a conversation.

A user’s selected state must reflect their own relationship to the publication. Removing a like or deleting a review removes that user’s contribution without deleting the publication or affecting another user’s activity.

### Save creates a reference in My Stories

Save places the publication in the **Saved Stories** view inside My Stories. It is a durable relationship between the user and the published story, allowing the user to find the story later without claiming it as their own work. The Gallery’s **Saved** feed is different: it ranks public stories by total saves from all users, while **Saved Stories** is the signed-in user’s personal collection.

The saved item must retain the identity of the creator’s publication. Unsaving removes only the viewer’s saved relationship. It must not delete the publisher’s source, unpublish the story, or erase engagement belonging to other users.

### Editable permits a separate owned version

When the publisher allows edits, the purple pencil communicates that another user may create a custom version. This indicator is not an Edit command inside Community Gallery and does not make the published story writable. The user saves the publication, finds it in **My Stories → Saved Stories**, and chooses the edit path there. Chronicle then creates a separate user-owned copy before Story Builder opens.

The resulting copy receives its own identity and ownership. Changes to that copy belong to the new owner and must not alter the original creator’s story or public publication. Chronicle must preserve the original creator’s attribution and the source relationship while keeping the editable object independent. If the user later publishes their version, it must be represented as a derived work rather than falsely presented as the original creation.

### Play begins a new conversation

Play is the Gallery’s primary handoff into the roleplay experience. After any required authentication and adult-content approval, Chronicle loads the selected story, uses its opening state to create a fresh conversation, and transfers control to Chat Interface. The conversation belongs to the player’s account and develops independently from the public publication, the creator’s story, and every other player’s conversation.

Starting a session does not save an editable copy of the story and does not change the publisher’s source. The Gallery records public play engagement; Chat Interface owns the live exchange. The resulting playthrough is stored as its own conversation in Chat History so the user can leave and later resume that same conversation rather than restarting the story. If Chronicle cannot preserve the new conversation for later use, it must not quietly present the session as safely resumable.

## Continuity, Feedback, and Recovery

### Loading should preserve the user’s orientation

The initial catalog, additional result pages, story details, characters, reviews, and personal interaction state may become available at different times. Pending work should be represented as loading rather than as missing content. When more results are requested, publications already loaded should remain available so the user does not lose their place.

Pagination is part of one continuous discovery session. Reaching the end should be distinguishable from waiting for another page, and returning from Story Detail should preserve the catalog state that led the user there.

### Empty results should explain what is empty

An empty unfiltered catalog, a search with no matches, and a Following view with no eligible creator publications are different user situations. A request failure is a fourth and different situation; it must not be presented as proof that no stories exist or that the user has reached the end. The Gallery should identify which situation occurred and give the user an appropriate next step, such as broadening the criteria, clearing the search, following creators, retrying a failed request, or returning later for newly published work.

### Interrupted actions must not imply completion

Canceling authentication or age confirmation leaves the requested action incomplete. A failed Like, Save, Review, Play, or unpublish request must preserve the user’s browsing context and must not be presented as a durable success. Retrying an action should operate on the same identified publication rather than whichever result happens to be visible later.

These recovery rules protect both user trust and ownership. The Gallery may update presentation while work is pending, but the lasting outcome must correspond to the account-linked or publication-linked record that actually succeeded.

## Handoffs to the Rest of Chronicle

### My Stories owns saved publications and the customization path

After Save, My Stories becomes the place where the user finds and manages the publication under **Saved Stories**. My Stories distinguishes saved publications from stories the user owns and handles later actions such as unsaving or beginning the permitted customization path.

When customization is permitted and the user chooses Edit from that saved item, Chronicle creates a separate owned story before Story Builder receives it. From that point forward, Story Builder owns editing and persistence for the new copy. Community Gallery continues to represent the creator’s original publication. This is an indirect handoff: Community Gallery sends the saved publication to My Stories, and My Stories begins the transfer to Story Builder.

### Chat Interface and Chat History own the playthrough

After Play creates and selects a fresh conversation, Chat Interface owns live roleplay, messages, story state, and response generation. Chat History stores that playthrough as a separate conversation and owns later discovery and resumption. Community Gallery remains the discovery origin and public engagement surface; it does not manage the conversation after the handoff.

### Public Profile owns the creator relationship

Selecting the creator transfers the user to the public profile experience, where the creator’s public identity, published work, and follow relationship can be explored. Community Gallery may use that follow relationship to shape discovery, but Public Profile owns the act of following or unfollowing the creator.

### The Gallery’s responsibility ends with a clear transfer

Community Gallery owns public discovery, evaluation, publication engagement, adult-content presentation, and the immediate initiation of each handoff. It must send the correct story or creator identity and make the result of the originating action clear. The destination capability then owns the longer lifecycle: My Stories owns the saved relationship, Story Builder owns the editable copy, Chat Interface owns the session, Chat History owns resume, and Public Profile owns the creator relationship.

The capability is successful when a user can find an appropriate story, understand what it contains, know exactly what each available action will do, and move into the next Chronicle experience without losing identity, ownership, privacy, or context.

# UI Elements and Functions

This tab defines the visible Community Gallery contract: what each interface element communicates, what happens when a person uses it, and what loading, empty, failure, responsive, and accessibility states must preserve. App Architecture explains how the current code produces these experiences, while Issues records any verified difference between this contract and the implementation.

## Gallery Navigation and Discovery

### Community Gallery Workspace and Access

Community Gallery is the public discovery workspace at Chronicle’s root page. The application sidebar identifies it with a globe icon and the label “Community Gallery.” The selected row uses the same filled blue-gray treatment as the other active Chronicle workspaces. When the sidebar is collapsed, the globe remains visible and its tooltip supplies the page name.

Visitors can browse, search, filter, open Story Detail, read reviews, and visit creator profiles without signing in. Like, Save, and writing or changing a review require an account. Following a creator is handled after the user opens Public Profile. If a visitor requests an account-linked action while signed out, Chronicle opens Sign In without changing the publication or pretending that the action succeeded.

Play has a separate guest presentation. Selecting Play while signed out carries the selected story into a read-only Chat Interface preview and then presents Sign In before the visitor can send a message or create a saved playthrough. The selected story must remain clear throughout that handoff.

### Gallery Feed Views

The top bar repeats the “Community Gallery” heading and contains six feed choices:

-   **All Stories** is selected initially and presents the broad eligible publication catalog.
-   **Recent** emphasizes newly published stories.
-   **Liked** ranks stories by their total number of likes. It is a popularity view, not the signed-in user’s personal liked list.
-   **Saved** ranks stories by total saves across all viewers. The user’s own saved collection is located in My Stories.
-   **Played** ranks stories by total play count.
-   **Following** presents the newest stories from creators followed by the signed-in viewer. A guest is asked to sign in, while a signed-in viewer who follows no eligible creators receives the Following-specific empty state rather than an unrelated general feed.

One feed choice is always selected. Its filled surface and white text communicate the active view; the remaining choices use muted text until hovered. Changing the feed does not clear the search field, hashtags, or category selections. On narrower screens the selector scrolls horizontally rather than removing choices.

### Search Controls

The sticky control row above the results contains:

-   **Browse Categories**, with a four-square grid icon, text label, selection-count badge, and chevron. Opening it adds the category controls beside the results. Selecting it again closes them without clearing anything.
-   A search field with a magnifying-glass icon and the placeholder “Search titles, descriptions, or #tags.” Ordinary words search titles and descriptions. Tokens beginning with a hash mark become lowercase publication-tag filters.
-   A circular X labeled “Clear search,” shown only when the field contains text. It clears the visible input, the applied text phrase, and all hashtags together.

Typing updates the results after a short pause of approximately 220 milliseconds. Pressing Enter applies the current value immediately. Search, hashtags, category selections, and the chosen Gallery feed remain active together.

### Browse Categories Panel

The category lane is divided into four groups:

-   **Story Type** begins open and contains SFW with a green shield and NSFW with an orange flame. The NSFW row is available only while adult content is being shown.
-   **Genre** begins open and contains Fictional, Fantasy, Romance, Dark Romance, Why Choose, Reverse Harem, Gothic Romance, Paranormal Romance, Enemies To Lovers, Hentai, Anime, Royalty, Action, Adventure, Religious, Historical, Sci-Fi, Horror, FanFiction, Philosophy, Political, Detective, and Manga. Each row includes an icon associated with its theme.
-   **Origin** begins closed and contains Original, Game, Movie, and Novel, represented by pen, controller, film, and book icons.
-   **Trigger Warnings** begins closed. Every row uses an amber warning icon. The current labels are Cheating, Cuckold, CNC, NTR, Chastity, Hypno, BDSM, Voyeurism, Bondage, Impregnation, Sissification, Breeding, Femdom, Gore, Bloodplay, Forced Orgasm, Humiliation, Drug Use, Coercion / Manipulation, Blackmail, Somnophilia, Captivity, Physical Abuse, Domestic Violence, Murder, Stalking, Isolation Control, Medical Play, Age Gap, Incest, Pseudo-Incest, Degradation, Breath Play, Knife Play, Free Use, Self Harm, Eating Disorders, Mental Illness, and Dark Themes.

Group headings use right and downward chevrons to show whether their rows are closed or open. Closing a group hides its rows but keeps its selections. A selected row gains a blue-gray tinted background, border, lighter text, and colored icon. Selecting it again removes it. Multiple choices within one group match any selected value; selections across different groups must all be satisfied.

When at least one category row is selected, the lane displays the active count and a category-only “Clear all” action. The label uses the correct singular or plural form. Closing the lane preserves the count on the Browse Categories button.

### Applied Filter Row

A separate “Filtering by:” row appears above the story grid whenever search or category criteria are active. It contains:

-   The text-search phrase in quotation marks.
-   Lowercase hashtag values without their hash marks.
-   Blue Story Type pills, purple Genre pills, green Origin pills, and amber Trigger Warning pills.
-   An X on each pill that removes only that criterion.
-   An underlined “Clear all” action that resets search text, hashtags, and every category selection together.

Each X is identified by the criterion it removes. Removing a hashtag pill must also remove that hashtag from the search field’s active value so the same criterion is not silently reapplied during the next edit.

## Story Listings and Card Actions

### Gallery Results Grid

Gallery results are displayed as poster-shaped cards. The grid uses one column at the narrowest width and expands through two, three, four, and five columns as space becomes available. Opening the category lane reduces the available width and can reduce the number of columns.

### Information Shown on Each Story Card

Each story card contains:

-   A tall 2:3 cover using the creator-selected focal position. If no cover exists, a dark fallback displays the first character of the story title, or a question mark when the story is untitled.
-   A compact SFW or NSFW badge at the upper right. SFW uses blue text and NSFW uses red.
-   A violet pencil badge at the upper left when the creator allows custom versions. Its accessible name or explanatory tooltip identifies it as permission to create a separate editable copy, not permission to edit the creator’s story.
-   The story title on one line, falling back to “Untitled Story.”
-   Up to two lines of italic description, falling back to “No description provided.”
-   Eye, heart, bookmark, and play icons followed by the full view, like, save, and play counts.
-   A “Created by:” line using the creator’s display name, username, or “Anonymous.” This line is informational and does not open the creator profile.

Cards do not show genre, Character Types, Origin, Trigger Warnings, Custom Tags, publication tags, ratings, review count, or publication date. Most of that information is reserved for Story Detail.

### Hover Action Tray

Hovering a card enlarges the cover slightly, strengthens the card shadow, highlights the title in blue, and reveals a centered action tray. The tray contains Like, Save, and Play. The title, short synopsis, counters, and creator remain visible; hover does not reveal a second description or any additional story information. Selecting a quick action does not also open Story Detail.

Keyboard focus and touch interaction must expose the same three actions without requiring a mouse hover. The icon controls require accessible names so their purpose and current selected state can be understood without relying only on color.

### Opening, Liking, Saving, and Playing from a Card

The card actions work as follows:

-   **Open Story Detail:** selecting the card body opens the complete story view. An eligible signed-in opening records a view, and the displayed total must correspond to the view the service accepted rather than increasing on every repeated open. If the cover is hidden by the NSFW preference, age confirmation appears before Story Detail.
-   **Like:** an unselected heart is neutral and becomes rose on hover. A liked heart is filled white on a rose surface. The button prevents repeat input and communicates that the request is pending. Success toggles the personal Like and adjusts the displayed total.
-   **Save:** an unsaved bookmark is neutral and becomes amber on hover. A saved bookmark is filled white on an amber surface. Success adds or removes the publication from the viewer’s Saved Stories relationship in My Stories; it never changes ownership of the original publication.
-   **Play:** the blue Play button records the play attempt and hands the story to Chat Interface, where Chronicle begins creating a fresh story session. A guest first receives the read-only Chat Interface preview and Sign In gate described above.

Like and Save require sign-in, while starting a persisted Play session requires sign-in. A failed action leaves the prior durable state intact, identifies what failed, and offers a sensible retry without making the user rediscover the story. The card body and quick actions must be operable by keyboard and touch as well as by pointer.

## Story Detail

### Opening and Closing the Detail View

Story Detail opens above the Gallery on a dark overlay. At desktop width it is divided into a cover-and-actions column on the left and a scrollable information column on the right. At narrower widths the columns stack. The X button, Escape, or a click outside the modal closes it and returns to the same Gallery view.

### Cover and Primary Actions

The left side contains:

-   A 3:4 version of the cover or title-initial fallback.
-   The SFW or NSFW badge and the violet editable pencil when those conditions apply.
-   Labeled Like, Save, and Play buttons. Like becomes “Liked” with a rose filled treatment; Save becomes “Saved” with an amber filled treatment.
-   “Remove from Gallery” when the signed-in viewer published the story. It removes the public publication immediately without a confirmation dialog. A spinner replaces its globe icon while the removal is pending.

The public version of Story Detail remains an evaluation and publication-management surface rather than a story editor. Editing the creator-owned source remains in Story Builder.

### Story Information and Metadata

The information side contains:

-   The story title in a blue-gray banner, with “Untitled Story” as the fallback.
-   A Story rating rendered with filled, half-filled, and empty amber stars when the aggregate is above zero.
-   A Spice rating rendered with red flames when the publication has eligible Spice ratings. The displayed aggregate must represent the publication’s complete eligible review set rather than only the review rows currently visible in the modal.
-   View, Like, Save, and Play totals. Values at or above one thousand are abbreviated with “k,” unlike the full numbers shown on cards.
-   A creator control with avatar or initial fallback and a “Created by:” label. Selecting it closes Story Detail and opens the creator’s Public Profile.
-   A violet “EDITABLE” badge when custom versions are permitted. Its tooltip explains that the story can be loaded into Story Builder from My Stories without changing the creator’s original.
-   A Synopsis section with preserved line breaks and “No description provided” as the empty value.
-   Genre, Character Types, Story Origin, Trigger Warnings, and Custom Tags when those fields contain values. Empty sections are omitted.
-   A Characters section showing up to eight circular avatars and names.

Characters display four avatar-and-name skeletons while loading. A missing avatar becomes an initial. If the story genuinely has no public characters, the section says “No characters yet.” Character items are informational unless Chronicle gives them a defined destination; presentation must not imply that an inert item is clickable.

Like and Save stay inside the modal and disable their own controls while pending. Play closes the evaluation flow and enters Chat Interface. Successful creator navigation and successful removal also close Story Detail. Failed actions keep the user’s context, explain the failure, and must not present an unconfirmed result as complete.

## Ratings and Reviews

### Public Review List

The public Story Detail view contains a “Reviews (N)” section. Guests can read it. Signed-in viewers also receive “Leave a Review,” or “Edit Review” when their own review already exists.

Each review contains:

-   The reviewer’s avatar or initial fallback.
-   Their display name, username, or “Anonymous.” A reviewer who hides public profile details is also presented as Anonymous.
-   A relative creation time.
-   An optional Story rating shown with stars.
-   An optional Spice rating shown with flames.
-   The optional written comment.

Reviews load newest first in groups of five. “See more reviews” appends the next group and becomes a spinner while loading. It disappears once Chronicle knows that no additional reviews remain. Initial loading, a genuinely empty review list, and a failed review request are separate states: the empty invitation appears only after a successful request confirms that no reviews exist, while failure provides a recovery action.

### Review Form

Leave a Review and Edit Review open a second modal titled “Rate This Scenario.” It contains:

-   **Quick Rating:** five interactive stars under “How would you rate the story?” The viewer selects an integer from one to five; there is no half-star input or clear button.
-   **Optional Spice:** five interactive flames under “How spicy is it?” This section is omitted only for stories explicitly classified SFW. A selected Spice rating cannot be cleared without replacing or deleting the review.
-   **Additional Feedback:** a closed section that expands to Concept Strength, Motivation / Tension, Worldbuilding & Vibe, Replayability, and Character Details & Complexity. Each accepts an optional one-to-five star rating.
-   **Optional Comment:** a multiline field with a 1,000-character limit and the placeholder “What worked well? Anything you'd want more of? This is optional.” No character counter is shown.
-   **Submit Review or Update Review:** disabled until either Story or Spice has a value of at least one. Category ratings and a comment alone do not enable submission. A spinner appears while the request is pending.
-   **Delete Review:** available only when editing. It opens “Delete your review?” with Cancel and a red Delete action before permanently removing the review.

### Submitting, Updating, and Deleting a Review

Edit mode preloads the saved values but begins with Additional Feedback closed. A successful submit or deletion closes the editor, refreshes the visible review rows, updates whether the viewer sees Leave a Review or Edit Review, and refreshes the public review count and aggregate ratings together. Submission and deletion failures keep the editor open, preserve the entered values, and explain that the review was not changed.

## Adult Content Visibility

### Show and Hide NSFW Switch

The right side of the application header contains one switch labeled “Hide NSFW” and “Show NSFW.” In the hidden state the thumb sits left on a dark track. In the visible state the track turns blue, the thumb moves right, and Show NSFW becomes the emphasized label.

### How Hidden NSFW Stories Appear

Hiding NSFW does not remove adult stories from the Gallery. It changes their presentation:

-   Existing cover images are enlarged slightly, heavily blurred, and darkened.
-   The NSFW badge, title, description, creator, counts, and card actions remain visible.
-   A card with no cover retains its unblurred initial-letter fallback.
-   The NSFW Story Type row disappears from the category lane, and any active NSFW Story Type selection is removed.
-   Like and Save remain available without age confirmation. Opening Story Detail or selecting Play requires confirmation first.

### Age Confirmation

When confirmation is required, Chronicle opens a centered modal containing a red “18+” mark, the title “Confirm Your Age,” an X close button, explanatory text, “Go Back,” and “I’m Over 18.” Go Back, X, Escape, or clicking the surrounding overlay cancels the pending action. “I’m Over 18” records confirmation, enables Show NSFW, closes the modal, and continues the pending Details or Play action.

Age confirmation and the Show NSFW preference are remembered on the current browser or device. Turning adult visibility off later does not revoke that confirmation, so turning it back on does not ask again.

## Loading, Paging, and Result States

### Loading and Empty Results

The Gallery uses the following result states:

-   **Workspace loading:** the Gallery workspace shows a recognizable loading presentation rather than an unexplained empty black area while its interface code is being loaded.
-   **Initial results loading:** four blank poster-shaped skeleton cards appear in the grid.
-   **Following is empty:** “No stories from followed creators” followed by “Follow creators to see their stories here.”
-   **Search or filters return nothing:** “No stories found” followed by “Try different filters or clear your search to see all stories.”
-   **The unfiltered Gallery is empty:** “No stories found” followed by “Be the first to publish a story to the gallery!”
-   **More stories are loading:** existing cards remain in place and centered text reads “Loading more stories…”
-   **No more stories are available:** centered text reads “You’ve reached the end.” There is no Load More button.

### Automatic Paging

Additional stories are requested automatically as the viewer approaches the bottom of the current grid. A filtered empty state keeps the active criteria visible and directs the viewer to remove individual pills, use Clear all, change the feed, or edit the search and category controls.

### Failure Presentation and Recovery

The Gallery must distinguish a failed request from a successful request with no matching stories. An initial failure keeps the discovery controls available, explains that the catalog could not be loaded, and offers Retry. A later-page failure keeps every card already loaded, explains that additional stories could not be retrieved, and offers a retry without changing the active search, feed, or filters.

The same rule applies inside Story Detail. Character and review failures must not be translated into “No characters” or “No reviews,” and failed Like, Save, Review, Play, view, or removal operations must not look successful. Recovery continues to target the same publication and preserves the user’s surrounding Gallery context.

## Responsive and Accessible Interaction

### Responsive Layout

-   The application header wraps when space is limited, while the six-option feed selector becomes horizontally scrollable.
-   The result grid grows from one to five columns.
-   The category controls remain usable without forcing the story grid beyond the viewport; on narrow screens they move into a layout that can be opened, reviewed, and dismissed without covering inaccessible content.
-   Story Detail changes from two columns to a vertical stack and keeps the cover, actions, close control, and information pane within the viewport at very narrow widths.
-   Quick Rating, Optional Spice, and Additional Feedback rows stack on narrow screens and spread horizontally when space permits.

### Hover and Persistent Selection

-   Hover effects communicate availability: card actions appear, covers enlarge, titles brighten, and category rows change surface color.
-   Filled hearts, filled bookmarks, tinted category rows, and the filled feed option communicate persistent selected state rather than hover.

### Keyboard and Accessible Naming

-   Every card body, quick action, feed option, filter control, modal action, rating control, and close control is reachable and operable from the keyboard in a logical order.
-   Icon-only controls have names that describe both the action and relevant state, such as “Like Acotar,” “Remove Acotar from Saved Stories,” or “Close Story Detail.”
-   Focus remains visible, opening a modal moves focus into it, closing returns focus to the control that opened it, and focus cannot move into the obscured Gallery while a modal is open.
-   Selected state, loading, failure, and content classification are communicated in text or accessibility state rather than through color alone.

## Delivery Boundary and Runtime Entry

### The Delivered Community Gallery

Chronicle delivers Community Gallery inside the main application route, `/`. `src/App.tsx` registers that route to `src/pages/Index.tsx`. `Index.tsx` owns the application-level workspace state and initializes the selected workspace to `gallery`, so Community Gallery is the first Chronicle area shown by the delivered root application.

`Index.tsx` lazy-loads `src/components/chronicle/GalleryHub.tsx` when the selected workspace is `gallery`. It also supplies the Gallery with the application-level values and callbacks that Gallery cannot own by itself:

- the selected feed and the function that changes it;
- the Show NSFW state and age-confirmation request;
- the authentication prompt used when a guest selects a protected action;
- the Play handoff into Chronicle’s story and conversation lifecycle; and
- the callback that refreshes My Stories after a saved relationship changes.

While that lazy-loaded module is being retrieved, `Index.tsx` supplies an empty black `div` marked `aria-hidden` as the Suspense fallback. It does not render a recognizable Gallery shell, loading label, or progress state during that workspace-level wait. This is separate from the card-shaped placeholders that `GalleryHub.tsx` renders after the module has loaded and the first publication request is pending.

The left application navigation is rendered by `src/components/app-shell/AppSidebar.tsx`. The Gallery feed selector and Hide NSFW / Show NSFW control are rendered by `src/components/app-shell/AppShellTopBar.tsx`. Those shell components display and change state owned by `Index.tsx`; they do not fetch Gallery publications or persist engagement by themselves.

### The Gallery Workspace Boundary

`GalleryHub.tsx` owns the discovery workspace below the application header. It coordinates search, category filters, the category panel, publication pages, the selected publication, Story Detail, Like, Save, Play, view recording, owner removal, and the signed-in viewer’s interaction state. It delegates durable Gallery reads and writes to `src/services/gallery-data.ts`.

When the category panel is open, the current workspace reserves a fixed 280-pixel column for it at every viewport width. The source does not switch that panel to a narrow-screen drawer or overlay, so the remaining search and result area can become compressed on small screens.

This boundary matters because displaying a control, holding browser state, requesting a database operation, and owning the durable result are different responsibilities:

| Responsibility | Browser or runtime owner | What it owns |
|---|---|---|
| Application navigation and selected Chronicle workspace | `Index.tsx` | Whether the user is in Gallery, My Stories, Chat Interface, or another main workspace; `AppSidebar.tsx` only renders the navigation control |
| Gallery feed and adult-content state | `Index.tsx` and `use-gallery-nsfw-preference.ts` | The selected feed, browser-local visibility preference, age-confirmation state, and pending protected action; `AppShellTopBar.tsx` only renders the controls |
| Discovery and publication interaction | `GalleryHub.tsx` | Search, filters, result pages, cards, selected publication, and Gallery action coordination |
| Gallery request boundary | `gallery-data.ts` | The client requests and response shaping used for publication reads, engagement relationships, counts, reviews, and publication state |
| Story session creation after Play | `use-index-scenario-lifecycle.ts` | Loading the source story, creating a conversation, and switching to Chat Interface |
| Saved-story lifecycle after Save | My Stories and the root scenario lifecycle | Showing the relationship, opening the source, and creating an editable copy when requested |

The deployed Supabase tables, functions, policies, and triggers—not `gallery-data.ts`—are the intended durable authority for accepted Gallery data. The repository proves what the client requests. The accessible Supabase reference documents the last synchronized backend account; an absent or consequential live definition remains unverified here. The Chronicle Work Manual's live-system safeguard governs any task that depends on that fact.

### Dormant Standalone Page

`src/pages/Gallery.tsx` contains a separate standalone Gallery page, but `src/App.tsx` does not register a route for it. It is therefore not the delivered Community Gallery and must not be used as proof of current user behavior. Its local feed state, authentication redirect, header, and navigation handoff are dormant source unless Chronicle deliberately registers and adopts that page later.

## Discovery State and Result Construction

### State Before a Request Is Made

The selected Gallery feed is application-level state in `Index.tsx`. Search text, parsed hashtag terms, category-panel visibility, selected category filters, the selected publication, and Story Detail visibility are local state in `GalleryHub.tsx`.

`GalleryHub.tsx` combines those values into one request description:

- ordinary search terms become `searchText`;
- words beginning with `#` become `searchTags`;
- selected story types, genres, origins, trigger warnings, and custom tags become filter arrays;
- the selected feed becomes `sortBy`; and
- Following adds a list of followed creator IDs.

The text field updates immediately as the viewer types, while the request terms are updated after a 220-millisecond delay or immediately when Enter is pressed. This keeps the visible input responsive while avoiding a request for every keystroke.

### Feed Translation

The header offers All Stories, Recent, Liked, Saved, Played, and Following. `gallery-data.ts` translates those choices before calling the backend:

| Header choice | Value sent to `fetch_gallery_scenarios` | Current source meaning |
|---|---|---|
| All Stories | `recent` | Requests the same sort value as Recent |
| Recent | `recent` | Requests recent-publication ordering |
| Liked | `liked` | Requests ordering by Gallery like activity |
| Saved | `saved` | Requests ordering by Gallery save activity |
| Played | `played` | Requests ordering by Gallery play activity |
| Following | `recent` plus publisher IDs | Requests recent ordering limited to the followed publisher list when that list is non-empty |

Liked, Saved, and Played are sent as sorting instructions to the public Gallery request. They are not built from the signed-in viewer’s personal Like, Save, or Play history in the frontend. The accessible Supabase reference describes the expected corresponding aggregation, but the exact live ordering remains a Lovable-controlled backend fact rather than something the client source can prove.

### Following Creator Lookup

When Following is selected while no user is signed in, the followed-creator lookup is disabled. The main Gallery query is also disabled because no followed-creator result is ever produced. No authentication prompt appears; the interface falls through to the ordinary Following empty state and tells the guest to follow creators.

When Following is selected and a user is signed in, `GalleryHub.tsx` queries `creator_follows` for rows where the current user is the follower. It extracts the matching creator IDs and adds them to the Gallery request. The Gallery publication query waits until that lookup has produced a value. However, an empty creator list is later converted by `gallery-data.ts` into no publisher filter. In current source, a signed-in user who follows nobody can therefore reach the general recent feed instead of an explicitly empty followed-creator result.

The lookup reads only the returned data and does not inspect the returned Supabase error. A failed lookup can therefore produce the same empty array as a successful zero-follow result, after which the unfiltered recent request can run. The interface has no separate visible failure branch for that lookup.

### Search and Category Filters

`src/components/chronicle/GalleryCategorySidebar.tsx` owns the visible filter controls and returns the complete `CategoryFilters` value to `GalleryHub.tsx`. `GalleryHub.tsx` is the state owner: it stores the selected arrays, counts the active selections, displays removable filter pills, and clears either individual values or the complete request.

The current active-filter presentation has three source-level limitations. The sidebar always uses the wording “filters active,” including when the count is one. The X buttons inside the active-filter pills have no accessible names. Removing a hashtag pill updates the parsed `searchTags` array but leaves that hashtag inside the visible `searchQuery`; if the user later commits the field again, the removed tag can be parsed back into the request. Clear search and Clear all update both representations, but the individual hashtag control does not.

When Show NSFW is off, `GalleryHub.tsx` removes `NSFW` from the effective story-type request even if that value was previously selected. Other NSFW publications remain in the result set and are masked at the card level; this story-type adjustment does not itself exclude every publication classified as NSFW.

The frontend includes `customTags` in its request model, active-filter count, and clear logic. The current category panel does not expose a visible custom-tag selection group, so that request field normally remains empty unless another caller or future interface populates it.

### Service and Supabase Request

`src/services/gallery-data.ts` is the Gallery data boundary. `fetchGalleryScenarios()` calls the Supabase remote procedure call (RPC) `fetch_gallery_scenarios`. An RPC is a named operation executed by the database rather than by the browser. The client sends the translated sort, search terms, filter arrays, publisher IDs, limit, and offset, and treats the returned value as the sanitized public publication result.

The frontend expects each `PublishedScenario` result to include:

- publication identity and source story identity;
- publisher identity;
- `allow_remix`, tags, publication state, and timestamps;
- like, save, play, view, review, and average-rating aggregates;
- public story title, description, cover, and cover position;
- public publisher display information; and
- content-theme information used for classification and filtering.

Two identifiers in this result serve different objects. `publishedScenario.id` is the public publication ID used by Likes, Saves, event counts, reviews, and remix lineage. `publishedScenario.scenario_id` is the source story ID used to load the underlying story for Play, publication changes, and later copying. Every interaction below names the identifier it passes because substituting one for the other can write against the wrong record or fail to load the story.

Hashtag search sends parsed tags as the RPC parameter `p_search_tags`. The older generated snapshot in `src/data/supabase-schema-map.ts` contains a stored `fetch_gallery_scenarios` definition that compares those values with `published_scenarios.tags`. The accessible Supabase reference documents the tags column but does not include that RPC body. The generated snapshot is non-live historical evidence, so the exact deployed comparison remains unverified here; the Chronicle Work Manual governs whether a later task requires a live comparison.

The exact live RPC definition is not available to Codex because the production Supabase project is controlled through Lovable. The accessible Supabase Schema Reference records the last synchronized account of documented tables and access rules. Any conclusion that depends on the current RPC body, deployed function permissions, trigger definitions, or undocumented database behavior remains unverified here; the Chronicle Work Manual governs whether the task requires a live comparison or reference update.

### React Query Cache and Automatic Paging

`GalleryHub.tsx` uses TanStack React Query’s `useInfiniteQuery` for publication pages. The query key contains `gallery-scenarios` and the complete request description. Changing a feed, search term, filter, or followed-creator list therefore selects a different cached result rather than mutating an unrelated result set.

Each page requests 20 publications. An `IntersectionObserver` watches a sentinel near the bottom of the grid and requests the next offset when the sentinel approaches the viewport. A page shorter than 20 entries ends pagination. A page containing exactly 20 entries permits one more request because the frontend has no separate total-count value.

Gallery results become stale after 30 seconds and may be refetched when the browser window regains focus. Chronicle does not maintain a realtime subscription to raw `published_scenarios` rows. The source comment explains that the subscription was removed so authenticated clients could not receive raw publication payloads that bypass the sanitized RPC.

Removal initiated inside `GalleryHub.tsx` explicitly invalidates the Gallery publication query. Publish, publication-setting updates, and unpublish initiated from `ShareStoryModal.tsx` do not perform that invalidation. Those changes depend on a later focus refetch, remount, another invalidating action, or other normal cache refresh before an already open Gallery is guaranteed to request the new publication state.

### Current Loading, Empty, and Failure Path

While the first query is loading, `GalleryHub.tsx` renders four card-shaped placeholders. While another page is loading, it preserves the existing cards and displays “Loading more stories…” near the paging sentinel.

Those states begin only after the Gallery module has loaded. The earlier React Suspense fallback owned by `Index.tsx` is an unlabeled black surface, so the current delivered path does not provide the recognizable workspace-loading presentation required by UI Elements and Functions.

If the resolved publication array is empty, the interface renders the ordinary empty result. Following has its own empty wording; other feeds choose between a filtered-empty explanation and the general “be the first to publish” message.

`fetchGalleryScenarios()` writes an RPC error to the developer console and rejects the request. Only a successful response containing no publications becomes an empty array. React Query can therefore distinguish a failed request from a legitimate empty or exhausted result.

An initial rejection keeps the discovery controls and current feed, search, and filters available, explains that the catalog could not be loaded, and provides Retry. A later-page rejection preserves the cards already loaded, explains that additional stories could not be retrieved, pauses observer-driven paging, and provides Retry for the same offset. Offline-paused requests use a waiting state rather than an empty or end state.

Changing the query clears the failure latch for the abandoned query. A retry captures the request description it belongs to and cannot clear a later failure after the viewer changes search or filters. A successful retry returns to the normal result, empty, or genuine end presentation appropriate to the returned page.

## Publication Cards and Story Detail

### Card Construction

`src/components/chronicle/GalleryStoryCard.tsx` renders one `PublishedScenario`. It receives the publication data, the viewer’s Like and Save membership, whether an NSFW cover must be masked, and callbacks for Like, Save, Play, and opening Story Detail.

The card owns presentation and local pending states. It displays the cover, title, shortened description, publisher, classification markers, editable marker, and engagement counts. Hover changes the presentation and exposes the action controls, but the card does not fetch the publication, write a Like, create a saved relationship, record a play, or open another Chronicle workspace on its own.

The current card body is a pointer-clickable `div` without a keyboard role, focus target, or keyboard activation handler. Its action tray is revealed by `group-hover` only; the source does not provide an equivalent focus-within or touch reveal. The Like and Save icon buttons do not expose accessible names or selected-state attributes, and the editable pencil is an unlabeled visual marker. These are current implementation facts, not evidence that the keyboard, touch, and accessible-naming requirements in UI Elements and Functions are delivered.

The card disables Like or Save while that request is running, which prevents repeat activation, but it does not change the visible label or expose an accessibility state that tells the user an operation is pending. Story Detail applies the same disabled-only behavior to its labeled Like and Save controls.

Selecting the card body delegates to `GalleryHub.tsx` to open Story Detail. Like, Save, and Play stop the card-open event and call their specific Gallery callbacks.

### Selecting and Opening a Publication

`GalleryHub.tsx` stores the selected `PublishedScenario` and opens `src/components/chronicle/StoryDetailModal.tsx`. When cached publication data changes, the modal receives the matching live cached record so Like and Save counts can update without closing it.

If the publication is NSFW and Show NSFW is off, opening details first goes through the age-confirmation callback. Approval turns on Show NSFW and then resumes the exact pending open action. Canceling discards that pending action.

For a signed-in user, opening Story Detail also calls the `record_scenario_view` RPC and immediately adds one to the cached view count. The code comment describes server-side 24-hour deduplication, but the local count increases before the server result is inspected. Guest detail opens do not call `recordView()` and do not change the displayed count.

### Detail Data and Ownership

`StoryDetailModal.tsx` is shared by Community Gallery, My Stories, and Public Profile. In the Gallery path, `GalleryHub.tsx` supplies publication metadata, interaction state, action callbacks, the source story ID, the publication ID, publisher identity, aggregate counts, and whether the signed-in user is the publisher.

When the modal opens, it separately loads the source story’s character previews and public reviews. These are not part of the original publication-page request. The modal also retrieves the signed-in user’s own review so the Review control can create a new row or edit the existing row.

Character and review read failures are written only to the developer console. The modal does not clear its previous character or review arrays before every new publication request. If a viewer switches publications and one of those requests fails, older characters or reviews can remain visible instead of a distinct unavailable state; a first-load review failure can also use the normal “No reviews yet” presentation.

The modal changes from a row to a column below the medium breakpoint, but its cover-and-actions section keeps a fixed 340-pixel width. On a viewport narrower than that fixed section plus modal padding, the source provides no smaller-width alternative.

In the Gallery path, `GalleryHub.tsx` does not set Story Detail’s separate `isOwned` display mode. The public publisher block therefore remains available whenever publisher data exists, including when the signed-in viewer is also the publisher. Selecting it closes Story Detail and navigates to `/creator/{publisherId}`. `src/pages/CreatorProfile.tsx` then owns the public creator profile, follow relationship, and that creator’s published work.

`CreatorProfile.tsx` renders Follow or Unfollow only when a signed-in user already exists. A guest can open the public creator profile from Gallery, but the current profile does not show a Follow control or open authentication for that action.

If the current user ID matches `publisher_id`, Story Detail displays Remove from Gallery. That equality check controls whether the client renders the action. The client depends on the deployed Supabase policy for durable authorization; hiding a button is not database enforcement, and this review does not establish the consequential live policy definition.

## Signed-In Interaction Flows

### Authentication Boundary

`GalleryHub.tsx` reads the current user from `src/hooks/use-auth.ts`. Like, Save, and Play require a signed-in user in the current Gallery implementation. When no user is available, the handler calls the authentication callback supplied by `Index.tsx` and stops before the Gallery service write or downstream handoff.

Opening Story Detail is not blocked by this handler, although NSFW detail can still require age confirmation. Review controls and owner actions are decided inside Story Detail from the signed-in user and publication owner values.

For a signed-in user, `getUserInteractions()` requests that viewer’s `scenario_likes` and `saved_scenarios` relationships for the visible publication IDs. The helper uses returned data arrays but does not inspect either query’s returned error. A failed membership read can therefore be represented in the current browser as an empty Like or Save set rather than as an unavailable personal state.

### Like

The Like flow is:

1. The card or Story Detail calls the Like callback supplied by `GalleryHub.tsx`.
2. `GalleryHub.tsx` requires a user and calls `toggleLike(publicationId, userId)`.
3. `gallery-data.ts` requests the existing `scenario_likes` row for the viewer-publication pair. It does not inspect that lookup’s returned error, so an unsuccessful lookup with no data can be treated as “not currently liked.”
4. It requests deletion of the row it found or insertion of a new row.
5. It returns the intended new Like state without inspecting the Supabase error returned by that delete or insert.
6. `GalleryHub.tsx` invalidates the cached interaction membership and adjusts the publication’s cached `like_count`.

The code relies on a database trigger described in source comments to maintain the durable aggregate count. The client changes its cached count after the service returns, so the visible card and open detail can update without refetching the full publication page. Because `toggleLike()` does not inspect the returned write error, a rejected database write can still reach the client’s apparent-success path. `GalleryHub.tsx` logs only an exception that is actually thrown; the current interface does not display or reconcile an ordinary returned Like error.

### Save and the My Stories Handoff

The Save flow uses `saved_scenarios` as a relationship between the signed-in user, the Gallery publication, and the source story:

1. The card delegates Save to `GalleryHub.tsx`.
2. `GalleryHub.tsx` checks whether the publication ID is already in the user’s saved set.
3. `gallery-data.ts` inserts or deletes the relationship.
4. The Gallery cache adjusts `save_count`, and the personal interaction query is invalidated.
5. `Index.tsx` reloads the saved-scenario collection used by My Stories.

Saving does not immediately copy the story or transfer ownership. It creates or removes a saved relationship. My Stories becomes the owner of displaying that saved entry and of any later Play or Edit action originating there.

The current service relies on the database to maintain the durable save aggregate. A thrown relationship-write error is logged, and the current Gallery does not provide a visible Save failure or retry. The later My Stories reload is a separate request. If the relationship write succeeds but that refresh fails, Gallery keeps its updated presentation while the My Stories collection can remain stale until another reload; `Index.tsx` only logs the refresh failure.

### Play and the Chat Interface Handoff

The Gallery Play flow crosses a capability boundary:

1. The card or Story Detail delegates Play to `GalleryHub.tsx`.
2. `GalleryHub.tsx` requires a signed-in user. An NSFW story also goes through age confirmation when Show NSFW is off.
3. `GalleryHub.tsx` starts `record_scenario_play(publicationId)` as a background operation. The wrapper does not inspect the Supabase error returned by that RPC.
4. It immediately calls the Play callback with both the source story ID and publication ID.
5. `Index.tsx` currently discards the publication ID and passes only the source story ID to `handlePlayScenario()`.
6. `src/hooks/use-index-scenario-lifecycle.ts` switches the application to `chat_interface`, shows a loading conversation state, and loads the complete source story through the persistence service.
7. The lifecycle creates a new conversation, includes the story’s opening assistant message when one exists, starts saving that conversation to Supabase, updates the in-memory Chat History registry, and gives Chat Interface the loaded story and new conversation ID.

`src/services/persistence/conversations.ts` upserts the new `conversations` row, requests deletion of existing `messages` rows for that conversation, and inserts the current messages when any exist. It checks the conversation upsert and message insert errors, but it does not inspect the result of the delete request, so the source does not prove that the old message rows were successfully removed before insertion. The overall initial save is fire-and-forget: Chat Interface can open before it finishes, and a surfaced failure is logged without returning the user to Gallery.

The lifecycle switches to Chat Interface before source-story loading finishes. If the story is missing or loading throws, it logs the failure, switches the root workspace to `hub` (My Stories), and clears the loading conversation identifier. The current path provides no visible Gallery-specific explanation or retry.

After the story loads, Chat Interface owns the live roleplay session. `Index.tsx` immediately adds the new entry to its in-memory conversation registry, but Chat History can discover that conversation after a reload only if the non-blocking initial conversation save succeeds. Community Gallery no longer owns message generation, conversation continuity, retry behavior, or later deletion.

The current handoff does not preserve the Gallery publication ID in the new conversation. It therefore cannot later identify that conversation’s originating publication from this path alone.

### View

The view flow starts only when a signed-in viewer opens Story Detail. `gallery-data.ts` calls `record_scenario_view(publicationId)`, whose source comment says the backend deduplicates within 24 hours. The wrapper does not inspect the Supabase error returned by the RPC. `GalleryHub.tsx` increases the cached view count immediately and does not reconcile it with whether the backend recorded a new unique view. The request asks the backend to record the durable event; the card-level cache change proves only that the current browser presentation changed.

### Current Detail and Rating Interaction Boundaries

`GalleryHub.tsx` passes `"Untitled"` into Story Detail when the selected publication has no title. That value arrives before `StoryDetailModal.tsx` can apply its own `"Untitled Story"` default, so the current detail heading differs from the required fallback documented in UI Elements and Functions.

Story Detail renders character portraits with pointer and hover styling but supplies no click handler or destination. The styling therefore implies an interaction that the current component does not perform. The interactive controls produced by `StarRating.tsx` and `SpiceRating.tsx` are native buttons, but they do not expose accessible names describing the rating value they select. The Story Detail close control also lacks an accessible name. These source facts identify where the delivered component currently differs from the stable interaction and accessibility contract; they do not redefine that contract.

Story Detail, Review, and Age Confirmation use the Radix Dialog root, portal, and overlay, but each places its visible modal inside an ordinary `div` rather than the repository’s `DialogContent` or `DialogContentBare` wrapper around `DialogPrimitive.Content`. The current source therefore does not receive that primitive’s dialog semantics, focus containment, Escape handling, or focus restoration. Review’s close control also lacks an accessible name; the Age Confirmation close control is named, but the surrounding content still uses the same ordinary-div construction.

## Reviews and Ratings

### Review Reads

When Story Detail opens, `StoryDetailModal.tsx` requests the first five public review rows through `fetchScenarioReviews()`. `gallery-data.ts` calls `get_public_scenario_reviews`, converts each result into the public reviewer, rating, comment, and timestamp contract, and returns the page to the modal.

“See more reviews” requests another group of five using the number of currently loaded reviews as the next offset. A page with exactly five rows leaves the control eligible for one additional request because the client does not receive a total count with the page.

For a signed-in viewer, the modal separately calls `fetchUserReview()` against `scenario_reviews` for the current publication-user pair. This owner-scoped row contains the fields required to reopen the viewer’s review editor; it is a different read from the sanitized public review list.

The modal’s displayed Spice average is calculated from the public review rows currently loaded in the browser. The publication’s Story average and Reviews heading count come from the aggregate values on the cached `PublishedScenario`.

### Review Create, Update, and Delete

`src/components/chronicle/ReviewModal.tsx` owns the review form. It passes the chosen Story rating, optional Spice rating, optional detailed ratings, and optional comment to `submitReview()`.

`gallery-data.ts` performs an upsert to `scenario_reviews` using the publication-user pair as the conflict key. An upsert means “insert this record if it does not exist; otherwise update the matching record.” The same operation therefore creates the viewer’s first review or updates the existing review. Delete removes the row matching that publication and user.

After a successful create, update, or delete, Story Detail reloads the public review rows and the user’s own review. It does not invalidate or refetch the Gallery publication page. The review list can therefore show the new row while the Reviews heading count and publication-level Story average still reflect the older Gallery cache until that cache is refreshed.

Because the Gallery path leaves Story Detail in public display mode, every signed-in viewer with a publication ID receives the Review control, including the publisher viewing their own publication. The current client does not apply a publisher self-review prohibition.

Submit, delete, first-page, and later-page failures are written to the developer console. The current modal has no separate review-loading state, so the normal “No reviews yet” presentation can appear before the first request finishes or after a failed request. Review state is also not cleared at the beginning of every selected-story load, allowing older rows to remain briefly while another publication is requested.

### Live Schema Verification Boundary

The current client permits a review when either a Story rating or Spice rating exists, sends absent detailed fields as `null`, and writes `raw_weighted_score` from the Story rating. The accessible Supabase reference describes that score as server-computed, while the generated client types in `src/integrations/supabase/types.ts` and the older `src/data/supabase-schema-map.ts` snapshot describe several rating fields as required that the current form treats as optional.

This is a source conflict, not proof of the live table definition. Codex cannot inspect the Lovable-controlled live Supabase project. Nullability, defaults, validation triggers, aggregate triggers, the conflict key, public identity masking, and current access policies therefore remain unverified live facts. The Chronicle Work Manual governs work whose conclusion depends on resolving them.

## Publication, Removal, and Editable Copies

### Publishing into Community Gallery

Community Gallery displays publications but does not originate the normal Publish command. Publishing starts in `src/features/story-builder/StoryBuilderScreen.tsx`. The builder runs `src/utils/publish-validation.ts` before opening `src/components/chronicle/ShareStoryModal.tsx`.

`ShareStoryModal.tsx` loads the current publication state, lets the owner set Allow Edits, and calls `publishScenario()` or `unpublishScenario()`. Publishing upserts `published_scenarios` with the source story ID, publisher ID, `allow_remix`, tags, and `is_published=true`. The ordinary Share Story interface currently always passes an empty tags array, so a publication created through this path contains no publication hashtags even though Gallery search can send hashtag filters. The accepted database row is the intended durable listing authority.

After the publication row succeeds, `gallery-data.ts` asks the `publish-cover` edge function to copy the private story cover into the public `covers` storage area. This cover promotion is best-effort. A returned or thrown cover error is logged and does not undo the successful publication row.

If the primary publication upsert throws, `ShareStoryModal.tsx` logs the failure and ends its pending state without a user-visible explanation or retry instruction. If it succeeds, the modal updates its own local publication state. The Gallery publication cache is not maintained by a raw-table realtime subscription and the Share modal does not invalidate it, so an already open Gallery receives the change only after a later refetch, remount, or other refresh.

### Removing a Publication

The owner action in Story Detail calls `unpublishScenario(sourceStoryId)`. The service requests an update that sets `published_scenarios.is_published` to `false` for that source story. It treats a non-error response as success but does not request a returned row or affected-row count, so it does not confirm that a matching publication row actually changed. It then invokes `publish-cover` with the unpublish action to request removal of the public cover copy. A thrown cleanup failure is logged, and the current service does not inspect the edge function’s returned error value in this path.

After the service resolves, `GalleryHub.tsx` invalidates the publication query and closes Story Detail. A thrown publication-update failure leaves Story Detail open but provides no visible error or retry; a non-error response that matched no row still follows the apparent-success path.

### Saved Story to Editable Copy

`allow_remix` is stored on the publication and controls the pencil indicator on Gallery cards and the EDITABLE explanation in Story Detail. Gallery itself does not contain a direct Edit or Remix command. The displayed instruction sends the user through Save and then My Stories.

When My Stories later receives Edit for a non-owned saved story, `use-index-scenario-lifecycle.ts` opens a copy confirmation. The lifecycle creates the destination story ID and passes it to `cloneScenarioForRemix()` in `src/services/persistence/scenarios.ts`. The clone helper assigns new IDs to copied characters, world entries, and scenes; explicitly makes scene images portable to the current user; removes conversations and side characters; and saves the resulting user-owned story. The inspected source does not establish blanket ownership-copying for every existing cover image or character avatar.

After the clone helper returns, the lifecycle attempts to write the source-publication and copied-story relationship to `remixed_scenarios`, reloads My Stories, selects the new story, and opens it in Story Builder. A failure to record lineage is logged and does not undo the new story.

The current My Stories clone path checks whether the user owns the source story, but it does not check the publication’s `allow_remix` value before allowing the copy. The flag currently controls Gallery presentation, not downstream clone enforcement.

## Adult-Content State and Gate

### State Owner and Persistence

`src/hooks/use-gallery-nsfw-preference.ts` owns two browser-local flags:

- `chronicle.gallery.showNsfw` records whether explicit Gallery material is currently shown; and
- `chronicle.gallery.nsfwAgeConfirmed` records whether the viewer confirmed being 18 or older on that browser.

`Index.tsx` uses the hook and supplies its current state to both the shell control and `GalleryHub.tsx`. The values are stored in `localStorage`, not in the user’s Supabase profile, so the remembered result belongs to that browser storage rather than following the account across devices.

### Visibility and Pending Actions

Turning Show NSFW on calls `requestShowNsfw()`. If the age flag is absent, `src/components/chronicle/GalleryNsfwAgeModal.tsx` opens. Approval stores both flags, closes the dialog, and resumes any pending Story Detail or Play action. Closing or choosing Go Back clears that pending callback.

Choosing Hide NSFW immediately clears the visibility flag and closes any pending confirmation. It does not delete the stored age-confirmed flag, so a later request on the same browser can enable Show NSFW without repeating the dialog.

When explicit material is hidden, Gallery still keeps NSFW publications in the result array to preserve grid density. `GalleryStoryCard.tsx` receives `maskNsfwCover=true` and owns the cover mask. Opening or playing that publication goes through the same age-confirmation request. This is a client-side presentation gate; it is not account-age verification and does not change the publication’s database visibility or access policy.

## State, Authority, and Persistence Map

“State owner” means the code that currently holds or changes a browser value. “Request boundary” means the code that asks another system to read or write. “Durable authority” means the source that should still be authoritative after a refresh or on another device. A cached count can change what the current browser displays, but it does not replace an accepted database row or backend aggregate.

| Information | Browser or runtime state owner | Request boundary | Intended durable authority and conflict rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Selected Chronicle workspace | `Index.tsx` React state | None | Session-local only; `Index.tsx` decides which workspace is rendered |
| Selected Gallery feed | `Index.tsx` React state | `AppShellTopBar.tsx` calls the supplied setter | Session-local only; the new state changes the Gallery request description |
| Search and category filters | `GalleryHub.tsx` React state | `GalleryHub.tsx` builds parameters for `gallery-data.ts` | Session-local only; the local values determine the next request |
| Publication pages | React Query cache in `GalleryHub.tsx` | `fetchGalleryScenarios()` | The accepted sanitized RPC result is expected to win on refetch; its live definition remains unverified here, and the Chronicle Work Manual governs whether a later task requires a live comparison |
| Selected publication and modal state | `GalleryHub.tsx` React state | None | Session-local only; a matching cached publication refreshes modal props |
| Signed-in user | `useAuth()` | Supabase authentication client | The Supabase authentication session is authoritative for protected client handlers |
| Like relationship | Interaction cache in `GalleryHub.tsx` | `getUserInteractions()` and `toggleLike()` | The accepted `scenario_likes` row and deployed policy are intended to win; the current client can display apparent success after an uninspected error |
| Saved relationship | Interaction cache in `GalleryHub.tsx` and the My Stories collection in `Index.tsx` | `saveScenarioToCollection()` or `unsaveScenario()`, plus the later My Stories reload | The accepted `saved_scenarios` row is intended to win; a failed reload can leave My Stories stale |
| Play and view events | Cached publication counts in `GalleryHub.tsx` | `incrementPlayCount()` and `recordView()` RPC requests | Accepted backend events and aggregate maintenance are intended to win; the local count does not prove deduplication or acceptance |

| Reviews | `reviews` and `userReview` state in `StoryDetailModal.tsx` | Public-review RPC plus owner-scoped review table operations | Accepted `scenario_reviews` rows and the public RPC shape are intended to win; failed reads can leave stale browser arrays |
| Editable-copy lineage | Copy lifecycle state in `use-index-scenario-lifecycle.ts` | `trackRemix()` | An accepted `remixed_scenarios` row is the intended lineage record; the copied story can exist when lineage recording fails |

| NSFW visibility and age acknowledgement | `useGalleryNsfwAccess()` | Browser storage calls | Browser `localStorage` is authoritative only for that browser’s presentation; it is not account or age authority |

## Responsibility and Handoff Map

### What Community Gallery Owns

Community Gallery owns public discovery, the current publication result set, public presentation, Story Detail entry, Gallery engagement commands, review entry, and the publication-removal request. It also owns the immediate handoff initiated by Save, Play, creator selection, or the editable-copy instruction. Removal remains inside the Gallery lifecycle rather than handing responsibility to another Chronicle capability.

### What Community Gallery Does Not Own

- My Stories owns the later lifecycle of a saved relationship.
- Story Builder owns authoring, publication readiness, and the later lifecycle of a copied story.
- Chat Interface owns the live roleplay after Play creates and selects a conversation.
- Chat History owns later discovery and resumption only after the new conversation has been persisted; the initial save does not block Chat Interface from opening.
- Public Profile owns the creator page and follow or unfollow changes after creator navigation.
- Deployed Supabase policies and functions are intended to own durable authorization, sanitized public reads, deduplication, and database-maintained aggregates when those rules are present and correctly configured.
- The Supabase Schema Reference records the last synchronized backend account; inaccessible live definitions remain outside this capability manual's proof.

### Identifier Handoffs

Community Gallery uses two different story identifiers:

- `publishedScenario.id` identifies the public listing and is used for Like, Save, Play count, view count, reviews, and remix lineage.
- `publishedScenario.scenario_id` identifies the source story and is used to load the story for Play, publication changes, and the later copy workflow.

Confusing those IDs can write an interaction against the wrong object or fail to load the story. The current Gallery Play callback passes both IDs to `Index.tsx`, but `Index.tsx` discards the publication ID before conversation creation.

## Diagnostics Versus Enforcement

These verbs describe what the current architecture actually does:

| Mechanism | Actual effect |
|---|---|
| `console.error` or `console.warn` | Records a diagnostic for developers; it does not explain the failure to the user, retry the action, roll back visible state, or block a later handoff |
| Card masking | Changes presentation of an NSFW cover; it does not remove the publication from the query or prove the viewer’s age |
| Client owner comparison | Selects whether to display Remove from Gallery; it does not authorize the database update |
| `allow_remix` label | Displays that the publisher marked the story editable; the current clone path does not validate that flag |
| React Query invalidation | Marks matching cached data for refresh; it does not itself prove a backend write succeeded |
| Local count adjustment | Changes the current card and modal presentation; it does not replace server deduplication or aggregate maintenance |
| Publish validation | Blocks the normal Story Builder publication dialog when required local fields fail its checks; it does not prove a successful publication write |
| Supabase row-level security (RLS) or server function | RLS is a database rule that decides which rows a user may read or change. It enforces the deployed backend rule only when that rule is present and correctly configured; the accessible reference records documented definitions but does not prove consequential live details |
